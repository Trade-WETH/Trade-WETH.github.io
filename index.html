Trade WETH.

When you trade WETH you are working with the ERC-20 wrapped form of ether that every major Ethereum trading venue, lending market, and perpetuals exchange treats as the base asset. Spot pairs, liquidity pools, and leveraged positions almost all settle in WETH rather than native ETH, so understanding how to trade WETH — not just how to wrap it — is what lets you actually use Ethereum's DeFi liquidity. This page covers the trading side: pairs and venues, supplying WETH to pools, posting it as collateral, and the liquidation and impermanent-loss mechanics that come with holding an active WETH position.

What It Means to Trade WETH

To trade WETH means buying, selling, pairing, and positioning wrapped ether as an active DeFi asset — not simply holding ETH in a wallet. WETH exists because native ether does not speak the ERC-20 interface that most smart contracts expect, so the moment you want to trade WETH against another token inside a pool, post it as collateral, or open a leveraged position, you need the wrapped version that behaves like any other token on the network. That one compatibility fix is why almost every serious exchange, money market, and perpetuals venue on Ethereum quotes and settles against WETH rather than the native asset.

Why WETH Instead of Native ETH

Native ETH moves as a protocol-level balance rather than a token balance, so a contract built to call the standard transfer and approve functions cannot handle it directly. Wrapping deposits ETH into a contract that mints an equivalent ERC-20 balance one-for-one, and that wrapped balance is what every pool, vault, and lending market is actually built to move.

WETH as the Base Pair

Most Ethereum AMM pools quote everything against WETH the way foreign-exchange desks quote against the dollar, because pairing every token directly against every other token would fragment liquidity into shallow, inefficient pools. A token paired against WETH inherits the deepest route to the rest of the market, which is why liquidity aggregators and Uniswap docs describe WETH pools as the default routing hop for swaps that touch ether at all.

Checking the Canonical Contract

Before you trade WETH on any new venue, confirm the pool or market actually references the canonical token rather than an unrelated contract with a similar name. The real WETH contract is verifiable on Etherscan, and matching that address against what a venue lists is a quick check worth doing once per new platform.

Where to Trade WETH: Pairs, Pools, and Perps

WETH trades in two broad structures. Automated market makers price it continuously against paired assets inside a liquidity pool, while order-book and perpetuals venues let you trade WETH directly against a counterparty at a quoted price, often with leverage. Ethereum.org's DeFi overview covers the mechanics behind both models in more depth than a single page can. Spot traders moving size typically route through AMM pools for the best execution price after checking depth first; traders wanting directional exposure without moving the underlying asset use WETH-margined perpetuals instead.

Supplying WETH When You Trade WETH Positions as Liquidity

Supplying WETH into a pool turns a trading position into a market-making one: instead of holding a directional view, you earn a share of every swap fee the pool collects, in proportion to your share of the pool. That income is steady when prices are calm and erodes quickly when they are not, which is the core trade-off anyone who wants to trade WETH through liquidity provision has to accept.

How Liquidity Providers Earn and Lose

Fees accrue continuously as other traders swap through the pool, but the pool's own pricing curve rebalances your WETH and paired asset against each other every time the price moves. You end up holding more of whichever asset fell and less of whichever asset rose — the opposite of what an unhedged holder would want.

Impermanent Loss, in Practice

Impermanent loss is the gap between what your pooled position is worth and what simply holding the same WETH and paired asset separately would be worth. It grows with the size of the price move since you deposited and shrinks back toward zero if the price returns to where you started, which is why it is a paper loss until you withdraw, not a guaranteed one. Checking a pool's historical fee yield against its volatility on a tracker like DefiLlama before supplying gives you a sense of whether the fees have tended to outrun the drawdown.

Using WETH as Collateral While You Trade WETH

Posting WETH as collateral lets you borrow against a position you still want to hold, which is how most leveraged WETH trades actually get funded on-chain. You deposit WETH into a money market such as Aave, borrow a stablecoin or another asset against it up to the market's loan-to-value limit, and use the borrowed funds to open a second position, effectively trading WETH with leverage while the original collateral keeps earning any supply interest the market pays. The trade-off is that your collateral is now tied to a loan: if WETH's price falls far enough relative to what you borrowed, the position becomes eligible for liquidation.

Liquidation Risk When You Trade WETH on Leverage

Any position where WETH backs a loan or a leveraged trade carries liquidation risk, and understanding how close you are to it matters more than almost any other number on the position.

Reading Your Health Factor

Lending markets express liquidation distance as a health factor or similar ratio, a single number that falls as WETH's price drops relative to what you owe. Keeping that number comfortably above the liquidation threshold, rather than running it close, is what turns a market dip into a manageable drawdown instead of a forced sale.

What Happens at Liquidation

When the threshold is crossed, a portion of your WETH collateral is sold, often at a penalty, to repay the debt and restore the position to a safe ratio. The sale happens automatically and is final, so the only real defense is managing position size and leverage before the price moves, not reacting after it has.

How to Trade WETH: Managing an Active Position

Running a WETH position well is less about the entry and more about the ongoing checks that keep it from becoming a liquidation or an avoidable impermanent-loss drawdown.
  • Size the position against your collateral so a normal price swing does not approach the liquidation threshold.
  • Pick the venue — AMM pool, lending market, or perpetuals exchange — that matches whether you want fee income, borrowing power, or directional exposure.
  • Check pool depth or order-book liquidity before entering, since thin liquidity widens the price impact on both the way in and the way out.
  • Monitor your health factor or margin ratio on a regular cadence, not only when the market is already moving.
  • Rebalance or add collateral ahead of a liquidation threshold rather than at it, since the forced-sale penalty is avoidable with earlier action.
  • Close or reduce the position deliberately when the thesis that opened it no longer holds.

WETH vs ETH for Traders

For a trader actively working positions, WETH is simply the form ether takes once it enters DeFi contracts, and the two are interchangeable at any time through wrapping and unwrapping. The practical difference only shows up in where value sits at a given moment: native ETH is what you hold outside contracts, while WETH is what the pools, lending markets, and perpetuals venues described above actually price, pair, and settle in. Traders who move frequently between spot pairs, LP positions, and collateralized loans end up holding WETH for most of that activity simply because that is the form the contracts require.
What does it mean to trade WETH instead of ETH?
It means interacting with the ERC-20 wrapped form of ether inside DeFi contracts — swapping it in pools, supplying it as liquidity, or posting it as collateral — rather than holding native ETH outside those contracts. Pools, lending markets, and perpetuals venues are built to price and settle in WETH.
Why do most AMM pools pair against WETH?
Pairing every token against WETH instead of against each other concentrates liquidity into fewer, deeper pools, the same way currency markets quote against a dominant reference currency. It gives any token the shortest, deepest route to the rest of the market.
What is impermanent loss when I trade WETH as liquidity?
It is the gap between your pooled WETH position's value and the value of simply holding the same assets separately. It widens as the price moves further from where you deposited and narrows back if the price returns, so it is realized only when you withdraw.
Can I use WETH as collateral to borrow against it?
Yes. Lending markets accept WETH as collateral and let you borrow another asset against it up to a loan-to-value limit, which is how most leveraged WETH trades are funded. The collateral remains at risk of liquidation if its price falls far enough relative to the loan.
How is liquidation risk measured on a WETH position?
Lending markets track a health factor or similar ratio that falls as WETH's price drops relative to the debt it backs. Crossing the liquidation threshold triggers an automatic, penalized sale of collateral to repay the loan.
Is trading WETH on a perpetuals exchange different from spot trading?
Yes. Perpetuals let you trade WETH's price directly with leverage and ongoing funding payments, without moving the underlying token through a pool, while spot trading exchanges the actual WETH balance against another asset.
Do I need to wrap ETH every time before I trade WETH?
Only once per batch of ETH you intend to use. Wrapping is a one-time step that converts ETH into the ERC-20 balance the pools and markets require; the wrapped WETH then stays usable across as many trades as you want without wrapping again.
